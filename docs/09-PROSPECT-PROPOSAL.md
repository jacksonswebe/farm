# 09 — Prospect Proposal (revised)

**This replaces the original 10-page deck for anything prospect-facing.**
The original is a good internal thinking document and a poor selling document. What follows is
the version to put in front of a buyer, and §1 explains every change so you can overrule any of
it with your eyes open.

---

## 1. What I changed and why

| # | Original | Revised | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens with a 14-module feature catalogue | Opens with one workflow and one number | The catalogue invites *"come back when you have permits and training."* You cannot win a feature-completeness contest against Intelex or Cority, and you do not need to. |
| 2 | Sells "EHS management" | Sells **audit-defensible evidence and accountable follow-up** | Nobody has budget for "digital transformation." They have budget for the thing that stops a regulator, an insurer, or a client audit going badly. |
| 3 | Calls itself an MVP throughout | Never uses the word | No buyer wants to be a test case. "MVP" is internal vocabulary. It is Release 1 of a product, and they are a founding customer with commercial terms to match. |
| 4 | 20 sections | 9 | A prospect reads two pages. Sections 11–19 of the original are procurement answers — hold them for the questionnaire, do not lead with them. |
| 5 | "Pricing to be validated through interviews" | Actual numbers | Refusing to price reads as *we do not know what we are worth.* Publish a number and discount against it. |
| 6 | Adoption never mentioned | Its own section | Adoption is the risk that actually kills this. Naming it first is what separates you from a vendor who has never watched a form go unused. |
| 7 | No statement of what it does not do | Explicit section | The fastest way to be believed about what you do have. |
| 8 | Roadmap as a promise | Roadmap as what the founding customer directs | Turns a liability into a reason to sign now. |
| 9 | AI positioned as a feature | AI positioned as a constrained assistant, with the limits stated | Overclaiming AI to a safety buyer destroys credibility. Stating the guardrails builds it. |

**The one thing I would not change:** the closing recommendation to demo a single complete
incident. That instinct was right, and it is now the whole shape of the document.

## 2. The document

### SafeSphere EHS
**Every incident investigated. Every action closed. Every record retrievable in under two minutes.**

---

### The problem you already have

Your safety records live in three places at once: a paper form on a clipboard, a spreadsheet of
corrective actions, and a WhatsApp group where the photos are.

That is survivable until the day it is not:

- An auditor asks for the evidence pack on an incident from fourteen months ago.
- A regulator asks what you changed after the last one like it.
- A client's HSE pre-qualification asks for your action closure rate.
- Something serious happens and the question becomes *what did you know, and what did you do.*

The problem is not that your people are careless. It is that the system asks them to be filing
clerks, and then holds them responsible for the filing.

---

### What SafeSphere does

One loop, done properly:

```
Report  →  Investigate  →  Find the real cause  →  Act  →  Verify  →  Close
   ↑                                                                    │
   └──────────────── and the system chases every step ──────────────────┘
```

**Report.** A worker reports what they saw from their phone in under a minute. No app to install.
Works with no signal — it queues and sends itself when the phone finds a network. There is also a
QR code for the noticeboard that needs no login at all.

**Investigate.** The investigator gets a structured workspace, not a blank document: sequence of
events, evidence, interviews, findings, and a guided 5 Whys. If a root cause blames a person, the
system says so and asks for one more "why."

**Act.** Every corrective action has an owner, a deadline, and a level on the hierarchy of control
— so you can see at a glance whether you are engineering hazards out or handing out PPE.

**Verify.** Nobody signs off their own work. Ever. If a control is judged ineffective, the system
raises the follow-up itself rather than hoping someone notices.

**Close.** An incident cannot be closed while an action is open. The full evidence pack —
report, photos, investigation, root cause, every action, every sign-off, and the complete audit
trail — exports in one click.

---

### The part most safety software gets wrong

**Software does not chase people. This does.**

Reminders at seven days, three days, and one day before an action is due. Overdue the day after.
The site manager at seven days. The executive at twenty-one. Automatically, every time, without
your HSE manager becoming a debt collector.

And when someone asks for more time, the original deadline is kept. Your on-time closure rate is
measured against the date first committed to, not the date last negotiated. That number stays
honest, which is the only reason it is worth having.

---

### Getting it used, which is the hard part

Most safety systems fail at the first step. Not because the software is bad — because a worker
standing in front of a hazard, in the rain, with one hand free, does not stop to log in.

Three deliberate answers:

| Barrier | Answer |
|---|---|
| No signal on site | The report form works offline and submits itself later |
| "I don't want to be blamed" | A QR code on the noticeboard — no name, no account, nothing traced back |
| "I don't have the app" | WhatsApp reporting, on the number everyone already uses |

We measure the leading indicator from day one: near misses and hazards reported per incident. If
that ratio is not climbing in your first ninety days, the product is not working, and we will
tell you so rather than show you a nicer chart.

---

### What it does not do yet

Stated plainly, because you will find out anyway:

- Permits to work, contractor management, training matrices and document control are **not** in
  Release 1. They are on the roadmap and your priorities shape the order.
- It is web-based and works on any phone browser. There is no app-store app.
- English only at launch. Swahili follows.
- It does not make you compliant. It gives you the records, the follow-through and the evidence
  that make demonstrating compliance straightforward.

---

### On AI

SafeSphere uses AI to remove typing, not to make safety decisions.

It drafts investigation summaries, suggests a classification, proposes the next "why," and offers
candidate corrective actions. Every one of those is a suggestion your HSE manager edits or
discards, and every one is logged.

It will never decide a root cause, close an action, or sign anything off. In a safety
management system, accountability has to belong to a person — and it does.

## 3. How to use this in the room

1. **Do not send this ahead.** Demo first, leave it behind. A document read alone becomes a
   feature comparison; a demo watched together becomes a decision.
2. **Lead with the auditor question.** *"How long would it take you, right now, to produce
   everything you have on an incident from last March?"* Then be quiet. The answer is the sale.
3. **Never open the roadmap unprompted.** When asked, answer: *"Not in Release 1 — and founding
   customers decide the order. What would you put first?"* You have learned their priority and
   made the gap a reason to sign.
4. **The three-places-only scarcity has to be real.** If you take a fourth, everything else you
   say gets discounted too.
5. **Discount implementation, never the subscription.** A discounted subscription resets your
   price permanently. A discounted setup fee is one concession that expires.
6. **Take the demo to consultants and ISO 45001 auditors, not only to end customers.** They sit
   with twenty organisations that have this exact problem and get asked "what should we use?"
   constantly. It is the highest-leverage room you can be in.
